# Writing and annotating lessons

Lessons are plain text files in `src/content/texts/`. You write Chinese and an English translation per line; `tools/annotate.py` adds words, pinyin, glosses and HSK levels.

```
@lesson id=h3-01 level=3 type=dialogue topic=Work minutes=3 source=Original
@title 面试
@titleen A Job Interview
@summary A young graduate is interviewed for her first office job.
@speakers 经理 小张
经理: 请坐。先介绍一下你自己吧。 // Please sit down. First, tell us a bit about yourself.
...
G: 因为……所以…… ~ Gives a reason and its result. ~ 因为下雨，所以我没去。 ~ It rained, so I didn't go. ~ 因为,所以
Q: Why does she want the job? ~ She loves writing ~ She needs money ~ A friend works there
```

* `type` is `dialogue`, `story`, `article`, `poem` or `classic`. Dialogues need `@speakers` and a `Name:` prefix on each line.
* `level` is a hint only: the annotator recomputes it as the lowest HSK level covering at least 90% of the words. Poems and classics keep a fixed level.
* `G:` has five fields separated by `~`: pattern, explanation, example, example translation, and the word tokens to highlight. Every key must be exactly one token in the lesson text; `npm test` checks this.
* `Q:` is question, correct answer, then two or three wrong answers.

## Inline hints

| Hint | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `我/喜欢` | force a word boundary |
| `行[xing2]` | force the reading of the preceding N characters, where N is the number of syllables given (`还[huan2]`, `朝[zhao1]`) |
| `{词=gloss}` | override the gloss shown for the preceding word |

## Lists that steer the annotator

* `lexicon.tsv`: names and words missing from the HSK list (`word<TAB>pinyin<TAB>gloss<TAB>level`).
* `lexicon_cl.tsv`: classical readings and glosses, applied only inside poems and classics.
* `compounds.txt`: words that must stay one token instead of being split into smaller HSK words.

* `gloss_fix.tsv`: reviewed corrections to word meanings. The dictionary's first sense is often not the one a lesson uses (才 is "only then", not "ability"), so a line here replaces a meaning everywhere (`w`), in one sentence (`o`), in the HSK list (`v`), or picks the reading the list leads with (`p`). The header of the file shows the columns. A line that matches nothing stops the build, so the file cannot go stale. `tests/gloss.test.js` checks the key cases.

Running `python3 tools/make.py` writes `build/amb_report.txt`, a list of words where more than one reading was possible and how each was chosen. Skim it after adding texts.

## Checking a poem

Clone `chinese-poetry` into `vendor/` and compare your text with it by hand. The shipped poems were checked this way; one wrong character was found and fixed.

## Word levels

A word's level is its level in the official HSK 3.0 list (2021). Words the 2021 list leaves out but HSK 2.0 or the later revision carries (表示, 春) take that level. Plurals, reduplications and compounds that are not listed (孩子们, 试试, 好朋友) take the highest level of their parts. Everything else, mostly poem and classical vocabulary, is shown as "Beyond HSK". See `derive_level` in `tools/annotate.py`.

## Quizzes

Each lesson ends with comprehension questions, written in English in the lesson text (`Q:` lines) and in Chinese in `src/content/quiz_zh.txt` (one `@id` block per lesson, the same questions in the same order, `question ~ correct ~ wrong ~ wrong`). The Chinese is annotated with pinyin like any other text and shown first; the 译 button adds the English underneath. Reading hints work as in the lessons (`东西[dong1 xi5]`).

## Idioms (成语)

`src/content/chengyu.txt` holds the idiom collection shown in Words, 成语 Idioms and as the Idiom of the day on Today.
One block per topic (`@Topic`), one idiom per line, seven fields separated by ` | `:

```
成语 | literal meaning | what it means | background (story or usage) | example (Chinese) | example (English) | tier
```

- `tier` is 1 (everyday), 2 (common) or 3 (literary). The Idiom of the day stays at tiers 1 to 2 until the learner's level is above HSK 3.
- Pinyin is generated by the annotator, tone sandhi included. Pin a reading with a hint on the idiom itself: `马马虎虎[ma3 ma3 hu3 hu3]`.
- The example sentence must contain the idiom, and every word in it must be known to the annotator (it fails the build otherwise).
- Only state a story as history when it is the standard account (Han Feizi, Zhuangzi, Records of the Grand Historian, and so on). Say "was said to" for legends, or describe usage instead.
- The idioms are inlined in `data.json` (`CY`, `CT`); their characters are added to the stroke shards. Saved idioms use the same flashcard schedule as words, with the idiom's own meaning and example on the card.

## The dictionary

The Dictionary view (in the Words tab) reads `site/dict.json`, built by `tools/build_dict.py` from CC-CEDICT (`cedict_ts.u8`, fetched by `tools/fetch_sources.sh`). It runs as part of a full `python3 tools/make.py`, not with `--no-data`, and the result is committed. Pinyin is converted from tone numbers to tone marks, `[pin1 yin1]` cross-references inside meanings are converted the same way, and "CL:" measure words are split off into their own field. Entries are `[simplified, traditional, pinyin, [meanings], measure words]`. The file is fetched the first time the tab is opened and cached by the service worker. `tests/dict.test.js` checks the data and the interface.

## Handwriting search

The writing pad in the Dictionary view searches `site/hw.json`, made by `tools/build_hw.py` from the stroke centre-lines already in `site/strokes/*.json` (Make Me a Hanzi). Each stroke is resampled to 8 points and normalised exactly as `hwPrep` in `src/app/core.js` normalises what is drawn, so change both together. Matching pairs the drawn strokes with a character's strokes by the cheapest assignment (`hwRank`), so stroke order matters only slightly, and a missing or extra stroke is penalised. `tests/hw.test.js` checks it on simulated handwriting and drives the pad in a browser.

## Speaking

The Speaking drill (Practice) picks short sentences from the lessons at or just above the learner's level, skipping poems and classical prose. `speakCompare` in `src/app/core.js` takes the recogniser's alternatives, turns digits into Chinese numerals, and marks which target characters appear in the best alternative (longest common subsequence). Sentences of four characters or fewer must be exact; longer ones pass at 80%. `tests/speak.test.js` drives the drill with a stand-in recogniser, since real speech cannot be tested without a microphone.
